Score sources for the song library
==================================

The compositions below are in the public domain in the United States (published 1930 or earlier).
The note data in scores.js was converted from these published transcriptions. Where a transcription
carries a Creative Commons licence, the licence and source are credited here as it requires.

  Changes made: notes assigned to guitar strings and frets; Maple Leaf Rag transposed up a semitone,
  folded into the guitar's range and thinned to at most four notes at a time.
  The Entertainer and Für Elise folded and thinned the same way; Für Elise merges both guitar parts.
  Canon in D: the violin part is set as the three-voice canon (entries two bars apart) over Pachelbel's
  ground bass, which was added here. Bourrée: the marked repeats are written out.

Christmas songs (version 7.2)
  Full scores, from the Mutopia Project (https://www.mutopiaproject.org/):
    O Holy Night (Minuit, chrétiens), Adolphe Adam: voice and piano, ed. Léon Grus, typeset by
        Gérard Gréco, public domain. Set for guitar here: the vocal melody on top, the piano's bass,
        and piano notes that lie below the melody.
    Pastoral Symphony (Pifa) from Messiah, Handel: Chrysander edition (1902), Mutopia Messiah
        full score, Creative Commons Attribution 3.0. The string parts, folded into guitar range.
  Melodies and chord symbols from John Chambers' ABC collection,
  https://trillian.mit.edu/~jc/music/abc/Xmas/ :
    God Rest Ye Merry Gentlemen, Joy to the World, The First Noel (the refrain is played once
    rather than repeated), We Wish You a Merry Christmas, O Come All Ye Faithful, Angels We Have
    Heard on High, O Christmas Tree (O Tannenbaum), It Came Upon the Midnight Clear, We Three
    Kings, Good King Wenceslas, O Come O Come Emmanuel, Coventry Carol, Here We Come
    A-Wassailing, Auld Lang Syne; and Up on the Housetop and Jolly Old St. Nicholas
    (transcribed by Thornton Rose, 2001).
  From Paul Hardy's Christmas Tunebook, https://www.pghardy.net/tunebooks/ , licensed Creative
  Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0 (these three arrangements are shared under
  the same licence):
    What Child Is This, Hark! The Herald Angels Sing, The Twelve Days of Christmas (the tunebook
    gives the last verse; all twelve cumulative verses are built here from its phrases).
  Melody only, from the Digital Tradition (abc_dtrad), with chords added here:
    Away in a Manger (the Mueller tune by James R. Murray), O Little Town of Bethlehem (Lewis
    Redner's St. Louis tune).

The other arrangements in the library were written for this program.
