User's Manual

Concert Acoustic is a playable acoustic guitar. You can play it yourself with a mouse, keyboard or touch screen, or let it play songs from its library while you watch.

Getting started

The guitar lies across the screen with the neck on one side and the body on the other. The dark fretboard is where you choose notes; the area over the sound hole is where you strum or pick. Sound starts after your first click or tap.

  • Quickest way to hear it: press Space to strum, then press 2 to 0 to change chords and strum again.
  • To watch it play: click Song library, then click any song.

Playing by hand

Choosing notes on the fretboard

  • Press a fret: click or touch between two fret wires on a string. The note is the one a real guitar gives at that fret. Touching left of the first fret plays the open string.
  • Tap plays (Tools menu): when on, pressing a fret also sounds the note, which suits picking out a melody with one finger or a mouse. When off, pressing a fret is silent, as on a real guitar, and you strum to hear it. Turn it off when holding chord shapes on a touch screen.
  • Releasing a fret stops that note, leaving the open string ringing faintly.
  • Several fingers: on a touch screen you can hold as many frets as you have fingers. On one string, the highest fret held is the one that sounds.

Strumming and picking

  • Strum: drag across the strings over the sound hole. Each string sounds as you cross it. Faster drags are louder and brighter.
  • Pick one string: click or tap directly on it in the strum area.
  • Where you strum changes the tone: toward the neck is warm and round; toward the bridge is bright and thin.
  • Direction matters: dragging from the low strings to the high ones is a down-stroke; the other way is an up-stroke.

Techniques

  • Palm mute: hold the strip beside the bridge (marked PALM MUTE), or hold Shift. Notes become short and thuddy while it is held.
  • Hammer-on: while a string is ringing, press a higher fret on it. The pitch jumps up without a new pluck.
  • Pull-off: hold two frets on a ringing string and lift the higher one. The lower note sounds.
  • Slide: press a fret and drag along the string to another fret.
  • Bend: press a fret and drag across the string (up or down the screen). The pitch rises smoothly, up to about three frets' worth.
  • Muted strings: in chords that skip a string, that string is marked ✕ and gives a dead click when strummed.

Chord buttons

The row at the top holds ten chord keys, which start as Open, G, C, D, Em, Am, E, A, Dm and F. Clicking one sets the fretting hand for you; pale dots on the fretboard show the shape. It stays set until you choose another. Open clears it. You can change which chord sits on each key in the chord library (below).

  • Strum or pick as usual and you hear the chord.
  • You can still press frets yourself on top of a chord, for example to add a melody note.
  • Number keys 1 to 0 choose the chords from left to right.

Song library and player

Click Song library to open the list. Click a song to load it and start playing. The guitar plays itself: glowing dots show each note on the fretboard and the strings shimmer as they ring. Click anywhere outside the list to close it.

  • Filters: the buttons at the top of the library narrow the list by style (Classical, Folk, and so on) and by era.
  • Labels: each song says how much of it is arranged: Full piece (the whole published score, with its repeats where marked), Full tune, Full chorus, or a number of verses.
  • Songs with verses play the tune once for each verse, the way a singer would perform them, and the accompaniment changes between verses (picking in one, strumming in another) so the repeats don't sound the same. If you add the words in the lyrics window, separate the verses with a blank line and each verse follows its own pass of the tune.
  • Christmas songs: click the Holiday filter to see the 29 Christmas and New Year songs together. Most are carols with three verses: the first is usually picked, and one verse is strummed or played in a bass-and-chord rhythm for variety. Some are different:
    • O Holy Night is a complete guitar setting of Adam's song with its piano accompaniment.
    • Pastoral Symphony is the instrumental piece from Handel's Messiah.
    • The Twelve Days of Christmas plays all twelve verses, each one longer than the last.
    • Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy and the March from Tchaikovsky's Nutcracker are guitar arrangements of each piece's main themes, about a minute each. The Sugar Plum Fairy includes the harp-like run in the middle.
    • Auld Lang Syne is there for New Year's Eve.
    Turn on the backing band in the Practice menu to play along with a bass and drums, or switch on Jam mode and strum the chords while the computer adds a lead part.
  • Greyed-out songs marked "Score needed" are listed but not yet arranged.
  • Sources: hover over a song to see where its score came from.

The player bar

  • Play / pause (round gold button, or Enter).
  • Stop (■) returns to the start and silences the strings.
  • Loop (⟲) repeats the song until you turn it off.
  • Progress line: click anywhere along it to jump to that point.
  • Tempo: the slider runs from a quarter speed to one and a half times. The small 100% button that appears beside it, or a double-click on the slider, returns to normal speed.
  • Playing along: you can strum and pick while a song plays.

Practice: learn mode and the game

Open Practice in the top bar and choose how the player behaves when you press play.

  • Listen: the guitar plays the whole song by itself, as before.
  • Learn: the next note of the tune glows on the neck and a fainter glow shows the one after. Play it (press the fret, then pick the string, or with Tap plays on just press the fret) and the guitar adds the harmony and moves on. It waits for you, so there is no hurry.
  • Game: the guitar plays the backing and leaves the tune to you. A ring closes in on each melody note; pluck the note as the ring reaches the dot. Hits within a split second are Perfect, a little further off are Good. A streak multiplies your points, and you get a score and up to three stars at the end. Lower the tempo to make it easier.
  • Tab strip: scrolling fret numbers on six lines, one per string, with the tune in gold. The line on the left is "now". Turn it off in the Practice menu.

Looping a section

  • Move to where the hard part starts (play or click the progress line) and click Start here, then move to where it ends and click End here. Orange marks appear on the progress line and that section repeats.
  • Combine it with the Tempo slider to practise slowly, then speed up. Clear removes the loop. It works in all three modes.

Recording

Start recording captures everything you hear, your playing and the songs, with all effects. Stop and save saves a WAV sound file to your Downloads folder (on an iPad, the Files app). Up to ten minutes at a time.

Backing band

Under Practice → Backing band, choose Bass only or Bass and drums and every song gets a band behind the guitar. The bass follows the song's chords and the drums keep its beat. The band follows the Tempo slider, loops, the count-in and the game.

  • Drums: Automatic picks a style for each song: a waltz for songs in three, swing for the jazz standards, soft brushes for classical pieces and a straight beat for everything else. Or choose one style for every song: Straight, Swing, Waltz or Brushes.
  • Level: how loud the band is against the guitar.
  • The bass is played on a simulated bass string, like the guitar. Songs without written chords get a bass line from the lowest note of each bar.
  • The band rests in Learn mode, which has no clock. It also backs you when you jam with the computer (see Jam with the computer).

Your progress

The app keeps track of each song for you: how many times you have played it to the end, your best game score and stars, and whether you have finished it in Learn mode. It shows in gold under each song in the library, and Practice → Your progress adds it all up. A new best score is announced when the game ends. Reset progress (click it twice) clears everything.

Tools

Chord library

  • Tools → Chord library opens a panel with every root note and eleven kinds of chord: major, minor, 7, m7, maj7, sus4, sus2, 6, 9, dim and aug. Choosing one sets it on the neck straight away.
  • Under Put it on a key, click a number to place the chord on that chord key. Reset keys restores the original ten. Your keys are remembered.
  • Chords without an easy open shape use the lowest barre shape that works.

Capo

Choose a fret from 1 to 11. A capo bar appears on the neck and everything moves up: open strings, chord keys, the chord library and the songs. Fret numbers in the tab strip and learn mode count from the capo, as guitarists do.

Alternate tunings

Tools → Tuning retunes the strings. The string names on the neck, the chord keys, the chord library, the scale guide, harmonics and the songs all follow, so everything still sounds right.

  • Drop D and Double drop D: the low string (and in double drop D the high string too) down to D, for a deep low end. Good for songs in D and heavy riffs.
  • DADGAD: an open, ringing Celtic sound. Try chord key 1 (Open) and let it ring.
  • Open G and Open D: the open strings make a major chord, so strumming without fretting plays G (or D). Lay the Slide straight across any fret for a major chord there. Open G is the classic tuning for blues and rock slide.
  • Half step down and Drop C: everything lower, for a darker, heavier sound.
  • Chord key 1 (Open) always plays the open strings, so in an open tuning it is a chord. The other chords keep their notes and are fingered for the new tuning, which can put them higher on the neck.

Slide and harmonics

  • Slide: pressing the fretboard lays a metal slide across all six strings. Drag along the neck and the pitch glides smoothly with no fret steps. With Tap plays on it strums as it lands; otherwise strum with the other hand. Try it with the Resonator or an electric guitar.
  • Harmonics: white diamonds mark the spots over the 5th, 7th, 12th and 19th fret wires. Touch a string there for a bell-like chime. Elsewhere the neck plays normally.

Fretboard guide

  • Scale and Key mark every note of a scale on the neck: the key note in orange and the others in blue, each with its name. Nine scales are included, from major and minor to blues and the modes.
  • Note names labels every fret with the note it plays. The guide follows the capo, the instrument's tuning and the flip settings.

Rhythm

  • Metronome: clicks on each beat, with a higher click on the first beat of the bar. During a song it follows the song's tempo; otherwise it uses the Speed slider.
  • Count-in: one bar of clicks before a song starts from the beginning.
  • Pattern and Start pattern (or P): the guitar strums or fingerpicks whatever chord you are holding, over and over, at the Speed setting. Change chords with the chord keys or your fingers and the pattern follows. Choose from down strums, down-up, a folk strum, boom-chick, waltz, Travis picking and arpeggios.

Tuner

Tune a real guitar listens through the microphone; the browser asks permission the first time. Play one string at a time: the note name appears with a needle and how many cents sharp or flat it is. Green means in tune. Close switches the microphone off.

Jam with the computer

The computer listens to the chords you play and makes up a lead guitar solo over them, like a friend sitting in.

  • Open Tools and click Start jamming, or press J.
  • Play chords in a steady rhythm: the chord keys with Space to strum, your fingers on the neck, or a strum Pattern (P) to keep the rhythm going for you. The jam keeps time with the Speed slider in the Tools menu, so set it near the speed you play.
  • After a bar or so it works out your key and starts to play. A line at the top of the screen shows the key and the chord it hears.
  • It plays in phrases: two bars of melody, a bar of space for you, then a lead-in to the next phrase. On the strong beats it lands on the notes of your chord, so it follows your chord changes.
  • Stop playing and it waits; start again and it joins back in. Press J again to stop it.
  • Style: Melodic uses the notes of your key. Blues uses the blues scale, with string bends and a swing feel. Country uses bright major runs with bends.
  • Lead: the instrument the computer plays: an electric lead, a clean electric, a jazz guitar, an acoustic guitar or a mandolin. Lead level sets how loud it is.
  • With the band: if the backing band is on (Practice menu), bass and drums follow your chords as well, so you lead a whole band just by strumming.
  • Starting a song stops the jam.

Try G, C and D a bar each, or Am, G, F and E. For the blues, choose Blues style and play E, A and B7 (put B7 on a chord key from the chord library). Each new jam is different: the solo is made up as it goes.

Lyrics window

The Lyrics button in the player bar opens the words for the current song in their own window, which you can drag to a second screen. On a tablet it opens as a panel on the right instead.

  • Adding words: the app does not come with lyrics. Click Edit words, paste or type them, and click Save. Put each sung line on its own line and leave a blank line between verses.
  • Following along: the current line is highlighted and kept in view as the song plays. The timing is approximate, spread evenly across the song.
  • Text size: the A− and A+ buttons.
  • Where words are kept: in the Windows app, as a text file per song in a lyrics folder next to the program. In a browser or on a tablet, inside the browser's own storage.

Exact timing

Normally the words are spread evenly across the song. For exact timing, click Set timing in the lyrics window: the song plays from the start, and you click the same button (or press the space bar) once as each line begins. When the last line is marked the timing is saved with the words. To redo it, click Set timing again. Editing the words without changing how many lines there are keeps the timing.

Karaoke

Practice → Karaoke, or the Words button beside Lyrics in the player bar, shows the current line in large letters over the guitar, with the next line underneath. Click either one again to hide the words. It uses the same words as the lyrics window, and shows nothing for a song with no saved words.

View

  • Sound hole on left: flips the guitar left-to-right, for left-handed players or preference.
  • Low E at bottom: swaps which string is at the top of the screen.
  • Zoom: scroll the mouse wheel over the guitar, press = and −, or use the buttons. Zoom in for a close look; zoom out to see the whole instrument with its headstock and tuning machines, and further out to see the room. Playing returns to the normal view and Whole guitar shows the full instrument.
  • Proportions: the playing view is squeezed along the neck so frets and strings are both easy to hit. As you zoom out, the guitar stretches to its true shape.
  • Backdrop: Dark studio, a Living room with a fireplace, or Christmas: the living room decorated, with a twinkling tree, presents, a garland and stockings on the mantel, wreaths, poinsettias, lights on the bookshelf, a paper star and snow falling outside the window. A shake of sleigh bells plays as it switches on. Christmas (photo) is a lifelike picture of a decorated room instead, with the fire flickering, the lights twinkling and snow falling in the windows; it stays still when you zoom. Lights pulse to music makes the Christmas lights swell with the guitar in either one.
  • You can play at any zoom level. These choices are remembered.
  • Stage mode (Tab): hides the menus and the player bar so only the guitar is on screen, and fills the whole screen where the device allows. Click Show menus in the top-right corner, or press Tab or Esc, to bring everything back. You can keep playing, and songs keep playing, in stage mode.
  • Default view: arrange the view the way you like it (zoom, position, backdrop, flip), then click Set as default. The app opens in that view from then on. Go to it returns to the saved view at any time, and Reset goes back to opening in the playing view.

Sound

  • Volume: overall loudness.
  • Tone: how bright the pick attack and the ring of the strings are.
  • Body: how much of the wooden body's resonance you hear. Low values give a bare, direct string sound.
  • Room: the amount of room echo.
  • Separation: the left-right spread of the strings. Far left is mono; further right spreads the low strings left and the high strings right.
  • Status line: shows that audio is running and the delay the sound system reports.

Choosing a guitar

The Guitar list at the top of the Sound menu changes the instrument you hear. The picture stays the same, and chords and songs work with every choice.

  • Dreadnought (steel): the standard full-bodied steel-string acoustic.
  • Classical (nylon): soft attack, warm and mellow, shorter ring.
  • 12-string: every string doubled, the lower four an octave apart, for a wide shimmering sound.
  • Parlor: a small body, boxier and more midrange.
  • Jumbo: a large body with deeper bass and longer sustain.
  • Resonator: a metal cone in place of a wooden top: loud, nasal and metallic.
  • Baritone: the whole guitar tuned a fourth lower.
  • Bass: an octave below the guitar, dark and long-ringing.
  • Ukulele: a fourth higher with soft nylon strings and a tiny body.
  • Banjo: very bright with a quick, dry decay.
  • Mandolin: an octave higher with doubled strings.

Electric guitars

The same list has seven electric guitars. An electric has no wooden body sound; it is heard through a pickup, an amplifier and effects, and it sustains much longer. Each one loads its own effects when you choose it.

  • Clean electric: glassy and bright with a little chorus and spring reverb.
  • Country twang: a bright bridge pickup, compression and a short slap-back echo.
  • Jazz hollow-body: dark, round and mellow.
  • Blues crunch: breaks up when you strum hard and cleans up when you play softly.
  • Classic rock: thick overdrive with plenty of sustain.
  • Hard rock / metal: heavy distortion; hold palm mute for a tight chug.
  • Surf: clean, drenched in spring reverb with pulsing tremolo.

Effects

The Effects menu works with any guitar, acoustic or electric. Anything set to zero is switched out completely.

  • Pickup (electric guitars): Neck is warm and round, Bridge bright and biting, Middle in between, and Neck + bridge hollow and funky. Standard uses each guitar's own choice.
  • Gtr tone (electric guitars): the tone knob on the guitar itself; turning it down darkens the sound before it reaches the amplifier.
  • Wah type: Sweeps by itself, or Pedal you move, which shows a pedal on the left of the screen. Drag it up and down, hold W to rock it forward, or on a tablet click Use tilt and rock the screen.
  • Amplifier: gives the sound the voice of a guitar amplifier and speaker. Leave it on for electric guitars; distortion without it sounds harsh and buzzy.
  • Amp tone: darker to the left, brighter to the right.
  • Drive: from a slight edge to heavy distortion.
  • Compress: evens out loud and soft notes and makes them ring longer.
  • Wah: a filter that sweeps up and down on its own.
  • Chorus: a shimmering, doubled sound.
  • Tremolo: a steady pulsing of the volume.
  • Echo and Echo time: repeats of what you play, and how far apart they are. A short time gives the country slap-back.
  • Reverb: the kind of space: Room, Hall, Plate, or the Spring found in guitar amplifiers. The Room slider under Sound sets how much.
  • All off: switches every effect off.

Your effect settings are remembered. Choosing a different guitar replaces them with that guitar's own starting settings.

Bass, ukulele, banjo and mandolin normally have four or five strings. Here they keep the guitar's six-string layout, moved up or down as a whole, so the same chord shapes and songs play correctly. Songs sound higher or lower to match.

Presets

Under Sound, type a name and click Save to remember the current guitar, effects, pickup and sound sliders. Choose it from Preset later to bring it all back; Delete removes it.

Touch screens and tablets

  • Two hands: hold a chord shape with one hand and strum with the other. Turn Tap plays off first so placing fingers is silent.
  • Palm mute: rest a finger on the strip by the bridge while you strum.
  • Zoom: pinch with two fingers on the wood or the background, away from the strings. A double tap there switches between the playing view and the whole guitar.
  • Larger targets: pinch in a little if the frets feel small.
  • Hold the tablet sideways. In upright position a message asks you to turn it.
  • Stage mode: tap Show menus in the top-right corner to get the menus back.

Installing on an iPad

Open the guitar's web address in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Start it from the new icon. After the first visit it works without an internet connection.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • The tuner says the microphone is not available: allow microphone access for this page in the browser or iPad settings. The Windows app asks the first time.
  • Learn mode doesn't move on: check the glowing string and fret, and that a chord key isn't holding that string at another fret (choose Open). With a capo, frets count from the capo.
  • The game feels too fast: lower the Tempo, or loop the hard section.
  • The jam doesn't start: it needs to hear chords ringing. Strum a few times; it starts at the beginning of the next bar. If it plays out of time with you, set the Speed slider in Tools to match your playing, or use a strum Pattern.
  • The jam is in the wrong key: play the main chords of your song a few times round; it settles on the key within a few bars.
  • The band drowns the guitar: lower Level under Practice → Backing band.
  • A song sounds strange in an alternate tuning: the notes are the same but some move to other strings; choose Standard to hear the original fingering.
  • No sound: click or tap once on the guitar, check Volume under Sound, and check the device's own volume and mute.
  • Notes feel late: close other audio programs and other open copies of this app. Wired headphones or speakers respond faster than wireless ones.
  • Notes cut off when I let go: that is how a fretted string behaves. Use the chord buttons, or keep holding, to let notes ring.
  • A chord sounds when I only want single notes: choose Open in the chord row.
  • The view is stuck zoomed: View, then Playing.
  • A song sounds wrong: arrangements vary in how they were made; a few were read by eye from old sheet music and may contain mistakes.

About the sound

Nothing here is a recording. Each string is simulated as it vibrates: a pluck sends a burst of energy around a loop that loses a little brightness on every pass, the way a real string does. The pick position, palm muting, hammer-ons and bends all act on that simulation, and a model of a spruce-topped body shapes the result.

Song scores are credited in the library and in lib/SOURCES.txt. The 3D graphics use three.js (MIT licence).
