User's Manual

Concert Acoustic is a playable acoustic guitar. You can play it yourself with a mouse, keyboard or touch screen, or let it play songs from its library while you watch.

Getting started

The guitar lies across the screen with the neck on one side and the body on the other. The dark fretboard is where you choose notes; the area over the sound hole is where you strum or pick. Sound starts after your first click or tap.

  • Quickest way to hear it: press Space to strum, then press 2 to 0 to change chords and strum again.
  • To watch it play: click Song library, then click any song.

Playing by hand

Choosing notes on the fretboard

  • Press a fret: click or touch between two fret wires on a string. The note is the one a real guitar gives at that fret. Touching left of the first fret plays the open string.
  • Tap plays (top bar): when on, pressing a fret also sounds the note, which suits picking out a melody with one finger or a mouse. When off, pressing a fret is silent, as on a real guitar, and you strum to hear it. Turn it off when holding chord shapes on a touch screen.
  • Releasing a fret stops that note, leaving the open string ringing faintly.
  • Several fingers: on a touch screen you can hold as many frets as you have fingers. On one string, the highest fret held is the one that sounds.

Strumming and picking

  • Strum: drag across the strings over the sound hole. Each string sounds as you cross it. Faster drags are louder and brighter.
  • Pick one string: click or tap directly on it in the strum area.
  • Where you strum changes the tone: toward the neck is warm and round; toward the bridge is bright and thin.
  • Direction matters: dragging from the low strings to the high ones is a down-stroke; the other way is an up-stroke.

Techniques

  • Palm mute: hold the strip beside the bridge (marked PALM MUTE), or hold Shift. Notes become short and thuddy while it is held.
  • Hammer-on: while a string is ringing, press a higher fret on it. The pitch jumps up without a new pluck.
  • Pull-off: hold two frets on a ringing string and lift the higher one. The lower note sounds.
  • Slide: press a fret and drag along the string to another fret.
  • Bend: press a fret and drag across the string (up or down the screen). The pitch rises smoothly, up to about three frets' worth.
  • Muted strings: in chords that skip a string, that string is marked ✕ and gives a dead click when strummed.

Chord buttons

The row at the top holds ten chords: Open, G, C, D, Em, Am, E, A, Dm and F. Clicking one sets the fretting hand for you; pale dots on the fretboard show the shape. It stays set until you choose another. Open clears it.

  • Strum or pick as usual and you hear the chord.
  • You can still press frets yourself on top of a chord, for example to add a melody note.
  • Number keys 1 to 0 choose the chords from left to right.

Song library and player

Click Song library to open the list. Click a song to load it and start playing. The guitar plays itself: glowing dots show each note on the fretboard and the strings shimmer as they ring.

  • Filters: the buttons at the top of the library narrow the list by style (Classical, Folk, and so on) and by era.
  • Labels: each song says how much of it is arranged, such as Full tune, Full piece, Opening or One verse.
  • Greyed-out songs marked "Score needed" are listed but not yet arranged.
  • Sources: hover over a song to see where its score came from.

The player bar

  • Play / pause (round gold button, or Enter).
  • Stop (■) returns to the start and silences the strings.
  • Loop (⟲) repeats the song until you turn it off.
  • Progress line: click anywhere along it to jump to that point.
  • Tempo: the slider runs from half speed to one and a half times. The small 100% button that appears beside it, or a double-click on the slider, returns to normal speed.
  • Playing along: you can strum and pick while a song plays.

Lyrics window

The Lyrics button in the player bar opens the words for the current song in their own window, which you can drag to a second screen. On a tablet it opens as a panel on the right instead.

  • Adding words: the app does not come with lyrics. Click Edit words, paste or type them, and click Save. Put each sung line on its own line and leave a blank line between verses.
  • Following along: the current line is highlighted and kept in view as the song plays. The timing is approximate, spread evenly across the song.
  • Text size: the A− and A+ buttons.
  • Where words are kept: in the Windows app, as a text file per song in a lyrics folder next to the program. In a browser or on a tablet, inside the browser's own storage.

View

  • Sound hole on left: flips the guitar left-to-right, for left-handed players or preference.
  • Low E at bottom: swaps which string is at the top of the screen.
  • Zoom: scroll the mouse wheel over the guitar, press = and −, or use the buttons. Zoom in for a close look; zoom out to see the whole instrument with its headstock and tuning machines, and further out to see the room. Playing returns to the normal view and Whole guitar shows the full instrument.
  • Proportions: the playing view is squeezed along the neck so frets and strings are both easy to hit. As you zoom out, the guitar stretches to its true shape.
  • Backdrop: Dark studio or a Living room with a fireplace.
  • You can play at any zoom level. These choices are remembered.
  • Default view: arrange the view the way you like it (zoom, position, backdrop, flip), then click Set as default. The app opens in that view from then on. Go to it returns to the saved view at any time, and Reset goes back to opening in the playing view.

Sound

  • Volume: overall loudness.
  • Tone: how bright the pick attack and the ring of the strings are.
  • Body: how much of the wooden body's resonance you hear. Low values give a bare, direct string sound.
  • Room: the amount of room echo.
  • Separation: the left-right spread of the strings. Far left is mono; further right spreads the low strings left and the high strings right.
  • Status line: shows that audio is running and the delay the sound system reports.

Choosing a guitar

The Guitar list at the top of the Sound menu changes the instrument you hear. The picture stays the same, and chords and songs work with every choice.

  • Dreadnought (steel): the standard full-bodied steel-string acoustic.
  • Classical (nylon): soft attack, warm and mellow, shorter ring.
  • 12-string: every string doubled, the lower four an octave apart, for a wide shimmering sound.
  • Parlor: a small body, boxier and more midrange.
  • Jumbo: a large body with deeper bass and longer sustain.
  • Resonator: a metal cone in place of a wooden top: loud, nasal and metallic.
  • Baritone: the whole guitar tuned a fourth lower.
  • Bass: an octave below the guitar, dark and long-ringing.
  • Ukulele: a fourth higher with soft nylon strings and a tiny body.
  • Banjo: very bright with a quick, dry decay.
  • Mandolin: an octave higher with doubled strings.

Electric guitars

The same list has seven electric guitars. An electric has no wooden body sound; it is heard through a pickup, an amplifier and effects, and it sustains much longer. Each one loads its own effects when you choose it.

  • Clean electric: glassy and bright with a little chorus and spring reverb.
  • Country twang: a bright bridge pickup, compression and a short slap-back echo.
  • Jazz hollow-body: dark, round and mellow.
  • Blues crunch: breaks up when you strum hard and cleans up when you play softly.
  • Classic rock: thick overdrive with plenty of sustain.
  • Hard rock / metal: heavy distortion; hold palm mute for a tight chug.
  • Surf: clean, drenched in spring reverb with pulsing tremolo.

Effects

The Effects menu works with any guitar, acoustic or electric. Anything set to zero is switched out completely.

  • Amplifier: gives the sound the voice of a guitar amplifier and speaker. Leave it on for electric guitars; distortion without it sounds harsh and buzzy.
  • Amp tone: darker to the left, brighter to the right.
  • Drive: from a slight edge to heavy distortion.
  • Compress: evens out loud and soft notes and makes them ring longer.
  • Wah: a filter that sweeps up and down on its own.
  • Chorus: a shimmering, doubled sound.
  • Tremolo: a steady pulsing of the volume.
  • Echo and Echo time: repeats of what you play, and how far apart they are. A short time gives the country slap-back.
  • Reverb: the kind of space: Room, Hall, Plate, or the Spring found in guitar amplifiers. The Room slider under Sound sets how much.
  • All off: switches every effect off.

Your effect settings are remembered. Choosing a different guitar replaces them with that guitar's own starting settings.

Bass, ukulele, banjo and mandolin normally have four or five strings. Here they keep the guitar's six-string layout, moved up or down as a whole, so the same chord shapes and songs play correctly. Songs sound higher or lower to match.

Touch screens and tablets

  • Two hands: hold a chord shape with one hand and strum with the other. Turn Tap plays off first so placing fingers is silent.
  • Palm mute: rest a finger on the strip by the bridge while you strum.
  • Zoom: pinch with two fingers on the wood or the background, away from the strings. A double tap there switches between the playing view and the whole guitar.
  • Larger targets: pinch in a little if the frets feel small.
  • Hold the tablet sideways. In upright position a message asks you to turn it.

Installing on an iPad

Open the guitar's web address in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Start it from the new icon. After the first visit it works without an internet connection.

Tips and troubleshooting

  • No sound: click or tap once on the guitar, check Volume under Sound, and check the device's own volume and mute.
  • Notes feel late: close other audio programs and other open copies of this app. Wired headphones or speakers respond faster than wireless ones.
  • Notes cut off when I let go: that is how a fretted string behaves. Use the chord buttons, or keep holding, to let notes ring.
  • A chord sounds when I only want single notes: choose Open in the chord row.
  • The view is stuck zoomed: View, then Playing.
  • A song sounds wrong: arrangements vary in how they were made; a few were read by eye from old sheet music and may contain mistakes.

About the sound

Nothing here is a recording. Each string is simulated as it vibrates: a pluck sends a burst of energy around a loop that loses a little brightness on every pass, the way a real string does. The pick position, palm muting, hammer-ons and bends all act on that simulation, and a model of a spruce-topped body shapes the result.

Song scores are credited in the library and in lib/SOURCES.txt. The 3D graphics use three.js (MIT licence).
